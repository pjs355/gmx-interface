## Polymarket

**What it does:** The largest crypto-native prediction market by global volume. Trades settle in USDC on Polygon. The main international platform and Polymarket US are separate products with different availability rules.

**Best for:** Esports traders who want the widest market selection and deepest liquidity on CS2 and LoL. Polymarket covers multiple competitive titles including CS2, League of Legends, Dota 2, Valorant, Call of Duty, Mobile Legends, and Rocket League. On Tier 1 Majors, Polymarket typically carries the heaviest esports volume and tightest spreads among crypto books.

**Esports-specific features:** Map-level markets alongside series moneylines on major CS2 events. ClutchComet supports live match viewing while you compare Polymarket prices against other venues on the same row.

**Limitations:** The main Polymarket platform blocks the United States and 28 other countries from placing orders. Polymarket US is a separate U.S.-accessible subset. Requires USDC funding, which adds friction for traders not already crypto-native. Withdrawals settle in USDC; fiat conversion happens outside the platform.

**Fees:** Sports taker fee = C × 0.03 × p × (1−p). At 50¢ on 100 shares: **$0.75** ($50.75 all-in). Makers pay 0%.

**On ClutchComet:** Tradeable. Linked on **109** esports matched rows.

## Kalshi

**What it does:** The largest CFTC-regulated prediction market by U.S. trading volume. Kalshi reported **$23.8B** in notional volume in 2025. Fiat-native, no crypto required, available in all 50 U.S. states, D.C., and U.S. territories.

**Best for:** U.S. traders who want USD deposits, federal regulatory oversight, and a clean interface. Kalshi covers CS2 and major esports alongside politics, economics, weather, and sports. For traders who move between an esports position and a macro trade in the same app, Kalshi has the broadest regulated catalog.

**Esports-specific features:** Dedicated esports category with CS2 subcategory. Match-level books and tournament winner futures on major events.

**Limitations:** Esports depth on individual matches often runs thinner than Polymarket on Tier 1 CS2. Bid-ask spreads can be slightly wider on the same match. Kalshi's member agreement restricts 50+ countries outside the U.S.

**Fees:** Taker fee = round_up(0.07 × C × P × (1−P)). At 50¢ on 100 contracts: **$1.75** ($51.75 all-in). Makers pay 0% on resting limits.

**On ClutchComet:** Tradeable via DFlow routing. Linked on **39** esports matched rows.

## Limitless

**What it does:** An on-chain prediction market on Base with USDC settlement and a central limit order book. Cumulative volume claims range from roughly **$270M to $497M** depending on source.

**Best for:** Traders already comfortable with Base and USDC who want exposure to esports lines where Limitless has active liquidity. Limitless is smaller by matched-row count than Polymarket or Kalshi but can lead on specific crypto and esports contracts when its book is hot.

**Limitations:** Esports catalog is narrower than Polymarket's. Not every CS2 match on Polymarket appears on Limitless. Dynamic taker fees peak higher on CLOB buys than Polymarket sports fees at mid-probability prices.

**Fees:** CLOB buy peaks at **3.00%** at ≤50¢. At 50¢ on 100 shares: **$1.50** ($51.50 all-in). Makers pay 0% on limit orders.

**On ClutchComet:** Tradeable. Linked on **29** esports matched rows.

**Best for:** Traders in the BNB ecosystem who want prediction market exposure without opening yet another Polygon or Base wallet. Esports coverage is smaller than Polymarket or Kalshi but growing with BNB-native distribution.

**Limitations:** Fewer esports matched rows on ClutchComet (**5** esports rows linked). Chain and wallet requirements add setup friction if you are not already on BSC.

## Myriad, BetDEX, Forkast, SX.bet, and Hyperliquid

These five venues appear on ClutchComet's All Odds view when linked for a matched row. ClutchComet does **not** execute on them. They exist so you can line shop against the full market before routing to an integrated book.

**Myriad:** Crypto-native order book. Peak taker fee ~1.5% at 50¢ (~$0.75 on 100 shares). DefiLlama reports ~$228M cumulative DEX volume. Useful as a price reference when its feed connects.

**BetDEX:** Solana-based sports exchange built on Monaco. **1% commission on net profit** only (no entry fee). Licensed in Anjouan and previously under Irish Revenue Commissioners regulations. Prohibited jurisdictions listed in BetDEX terms.

**Forkast:** Esports-first prediction market on Arbitrum. Weekly volume in active weeks has run roughly **$100K-$110K** per industry reports. The closest thing to a dedicated esports prediction venue in this list. Promo zero fees through June 2026 on standard schedule per Forkast docs.

**SX.bet:** DeFi sports exchange. **0% maker/taker on singles**; 5% on parlay profit only. DefiLlama reports ~$668M cumulative DEX volume. **Not available in the United States** per SX Bet documentation. Shown as **SX** in the ClutchComet UI.

**Hyperliquid:** HIP-4 outcome markets launched May 2026. No fee on open; settlement tier-0 taker **0.045%** on $1/share. ClutchComet shows outcome-market prices on matched rows, not the full Hyperliquid perp stack.

If the best quote sits on a comparison-only venue, you at least know before you commit capital on a tradeable book. That is the point of showing all nine columns.

**Robinhood Event Contracts** expose Kalshi contracts inside the Robinhood app. Same underlying markets as Kalshi with zero additional onboarding if you already use Robinhood. No material advantage over Kalshi for standalone esports trading, and no Polymarket-style esports catalog breadth.

## Why "best platform" is the wrong question

No single venue is consistently the best place to trade any given esports match.

On a Tier 1 Major playoff, Polymarket often has deeper liquidity and tighter spreads. Kalshi may lag by 1-3 cents or lead depending on which trader community is active. Limitless or Predict can show the best integrated price on a specific row when their books are hot.

On a tier-2 CCT match with **$8K** in total volume, the gap between venues can reach **8-15 cents**. Which venue is "better" depends on which book has active traders on that circuit right now.

The traders getting the best results in 2026 are not picking one platform and staying there. They compare prices across active venues on every match before executing, or use a tool that surfaces all of them at once.
