Short answer: probably yes, depending on where you are, but the legal framework depends entirely on which type of platform you use.

Most guides on this topic give vague non-answers because they conflate two completely different legal regimes. Traditional esports sportsbooks (DraftKings, Betway, offshore sites) operate under gambling law, which is state-regulated in the U.S. and varies country by country internationally. Prediction markets (Kalshi, Polymarket US) operate under federal commodities law in the U.S., which means a different map entirely. This guide covers both.

**Last updated:** June 2026. U.S. prediction market status is changing quickly. This page reflects major public actions and court orders through mid-June 2026.

## The two questions you actually need to answer

**1. Can I bet on esports at a sportsbook?**

Esports betting follows the same rules as traditional sports betting in your jurisdiction. Wherever online sports betting is legal, esports markets are generally available on licensed platforms.

**2. Can I trade esports contracts on a prediction market?**

In the U.S., this is a separate question with a separate answer. Prediction markets are regulated federally by the CFTC, not by state gaming commissions. That means the state-by-state sports betting map does not fully apply. Most U.S. adults can access Kalshi today. Polymarket's international platform blocks U.S. users; **Polymarket US** is the separate U.S.-accessible product. A small number of states are actively litigating.

## U.S. deep dive: the prediction market situation

This is where most people are genuinely confused, so it is worth spending more time here.

### The federal argument

Kalshi and Polymarket US operate as CFTC-registered Designated Contract Markets (DCMs). Their position: sports event contracts are federally regulated financial instruments under the Commodity Exchange Act, and federal law preempts state gambling statutes where applicable. The CFTC has filed federal lawsuits against multiple states attempting to restrict the platforms.

### The state argument

More than 30 states have filed legal briefs arguing that sports prediction markets are functionally indistinguishable from sports betting and should be subject to state gambling law. States with established sportsbook markets see displaced tax revenue. One 2026 industry analysis estimated roughly $600 million in affected state revenue across contested jurisdictions.

### What that means right now (June 2026)

**Effectively blocked or soon blocked:**

- **Nevada:** Court-ordered ban in effect as of June 2, 2026. The 9th Circuit denied a stay May 22, 2026.
- **Minnesota:** State ban signed into law May 18, 2026; takes effect August 1, 2026. CFTC filed suit immediately. Outcome pending.
- **Utah:** Blanket gambling prohibition applies.

**Active litigation, but currently accessible:**

- **Arizona:** Criminal charges filed against Kalshi. CFTC won a federal TRO blocking the criminal case.
- **Connecticut, Massachusetts, Maryland:** Cease-and-desist postures. Platforms largely accessible under federal cover as of June 2026.
- **New Jersey:** Third Circuit ruled in Kalshi's favor April 6, 2026. Currently accessible.

**Accessible, no major state action:**

Most remaining states. If your state is not in the contested list above, Kalshi is generally accessible today. Kalshi markets itself as available in all 50 states, D.C., and U.S. territories, subject to ongoing litigation outcomes.

### The big picture

Prediction markets are widely expected to reach the Supreme Court. As of June 2026, prediction market contracts on whether SCOTUS accepts a sports event contract case by year-end trade near a 64% implied probability. That number moves with every headline.

## International: the cleaner story

Outside the U.S., the picture is more settled for sportsbooks, though U.S. prediction market platforms have not launched broadly internationally.

**UK.** Esports betting is fully legal and treated by the UK Gambling Commission the same as any other live sporting event. Kalshi and Polymarket are not licensed in the UK. Polymarket geoblocks UK users on its international platform. Matchbook has begun offering prediction-style markets under its existing betting exchange license.

**Europe.** Major markets including Germany, France, Italy, and Spain have licensed sportsbook frameworks that cover esports betting. U.S.-based prediction market platforms are not generally available.

**Australia.** Esports betting is legal via licensed operators. The Interactive Gambling Act restricts internet-based live (in-play) betting to phone or retail channels in some cases. Pre-match esports betting online is widely available.

**Canada.** Single-event sports betting was federalized in 2021. Ontario has the most developed licensed market. Other provinces allow betting via licensed operators. Esports is covered under standard sports betting frameworks.

**Brazil.** Online sports betting was legalized in January 2025. 2026 is the first full year of active enforcement and licensing, with esports included in regulated markets.
